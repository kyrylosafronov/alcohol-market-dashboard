From 2021 to 2024, value and physical volume diverged: prices, inflation, and premiumisation supported market value while beverage volumes were weak or declining.

How to interpret the production series

Beer. Ukrpivo provides an industry estimate for Ukraine; BarthHaas provides global estimates. Historical figures may be revised in later BarthHaas editions.
Wine. OIV excludes juice and must. Its 2025 estimate was updated from a preliminary 232 to a final 227 million hectolitres. Ukraine’s available series covers wine-making materials, not finished retail wine.
Spirits. Open data do not provide a comparable global production series for vodka, whisky, brandy, tequila, and gin. CRT reports tequila under its protected designation; IWSR’s global spirits figures measure sales, not production.

Market drivers by period

Demand, production, pricing, and statistical coverage are considered separately. These explanations interpret the cited sources; they do not estimate causal effects.

2021 · recovery

The global market rebounded from the pandemic-driven 2020 slump: sales rose 3% by volume and 12% by value. Before the full-scale invasion, Ukraine’s retail volume index rose 11.4%; beer production was about 1.705 billion litres.

2022 · shock and disruption

The global market grew 1% by volume and 6% by value as restrictions eased, while inflation and supply disruptions weighed on spending. In Ukraine, war, occupation, damage to production capacity and packaging supply, displacement, and trade and logistics constraints coincided with a 27.9% drop in beer production and a 32% fall in the retail volume index.

2023 · partial recovery

Global sales fell 1% by volume while value rose 2%, amid cost-of-living pressures and inventory adjustments. Ukraine’s retail volume index rebounded 24.3% from a low base; beer production rose 8.6% and wine-making materials 5.2%. This was a recovery, not a return to pre-war levels.

2024 · value outpaces volume

Global beverage alcohol volume fell 1% while value rose 1%, amid weakness in major markets and wine’s structural decline. Ukraine’s beer production rose about 4.9%, reaching roughly 82.1% of its 2021 level; wine-making materials production fell 38.1%. Euromonitor reports sales volume above 2023 but below 2021.

2025 · demand contraction

Global beverage alcohol volume fell for a third consecutive year (−2%). Preliminary value fell 4% across 22 markets at a fixed exchange rate; confirmed global 2025 volume data are now available. Ukraine’s beer production fell about 4.1% from 2024. Euromonitor describes declining total sales volume alongside rising value amid inflation, minimum prices, and cautious spending.

Minimum prices in Ukraine

The law was adopted on 10 October 2024; the new prices took effect on 1 December 2024. The 50% increase applied to wine products and some fermented beverages and distillates, including certain brandies. The State Tax Service table left minimum prices for vodka, whisky, rum, and gin unchanged. The effect should therefore not be attributed to the entire alcohol market or all of 2024.

Nominal revenue ≠ consumption

An increase in UAH or USD value may reflect inflation, exchange rates, taxes, minimum prices, product mix, or sales shifting from the shadow economy to the legal market. Physical volume is more informative about consumption, but it also misses unrecorded production and sales.

Key findings

  1. Global beverage alcohol volume peaked in 2022 and fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Market value continued to grow through 2024, supported by prices and premiumisation.
  2. Ukraine’s retail market was hit harder by the war: the physical volume index fell 32% in 2022, then rebounded 24.3% in 2023. Turnover rose even faster in 2023 (+39.1%), so this should not be read as an equal increase in alcohol consumed.
  3. Ukraine’s beer production partly recovered after 2022, then declined again in 2025 to about 21% below 2021. Wine and wine-making materials production faced additional pressure from vineyard losses, weather, and damage.
  4. Comparisons across beverages require separate series and clear caveats. Adding beer, wine, and spirits figures from different sources creates false precision; litres of product are not equivalent to litres of pure alcohol.

Sources and methodology

Official and industry sources are prioritised. Commercial estimates and incomplete series are identified.
